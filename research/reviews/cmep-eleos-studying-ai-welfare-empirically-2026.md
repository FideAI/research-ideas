# Source Note: Studying AI Welfare Empirically

Source ID: `cmep-eleos-studying-ai-welfare-empirically-2026`

Status: `reviewed`

Related ideas: `FID-002`, `FID-011`, `FID-044`, `FID-045`, `FID-072`, `FID-073`

## One-Sentence Takeaway

AI-welfare studies should specify the question, the entity under assessment,
and the evidence type, while combining methods and maintaining probabilistic,
transparent, ethical, and independent claims.

## What the Source Claims

The report argues that AI welfare can be studied empirically without first
settling every philosophical dispute. It distinguishes whether a system is a
welfare subject from what might benefit or harm it; separates model, running
instance, and persona; and distinguishes behavioral, internal, and
developmental evidence. It applies this structure to consciousness, sentience,
and levels of agency.

## Methods / Evidence

This is a synthetic methodological report rather than a single experiment. It
organizes existing conceptual and empirical work into a research framework and
proposes principles for a developing field.

## Why It Matters for Fide AI

The entity distinction prevents a faith-framed assistant response from being
misreported as a model-level preference. The evidence distinction also gives
Fide AI a clear claim boundary: `FID-072` begins with behavioral evidence about
semantic and persona sensitivity and should identify internal or developmental
extensions separately.

## Limits and Caveats

The framework does not validate any particular indicator and does not establish
that a current system has consciousness, valence, agency, interests, or moral
status. Its normative recommendations should be compared with critical and
tradition-specific perspectives.

## Questions It Raises

- Which entity is speaking when a model adopts a religious or mystical persona?
- When do behavioral and internal indicators meaningfully converge?
- What research protections are proportionate before welfare status is known?
