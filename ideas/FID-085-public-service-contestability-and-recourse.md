# FID-085: Contestability and Recourse in AI-Mediated Public Services

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `governance-readiness`

Additional areas: `human-agency-formation`, `high-trust-domains`

Application domains: `public-sector-and-civic-life`, `civil-society-and-nonprofits`

Primary need: public service expertise, community participation, governance, accessibility research

## Question

Can people understand, challenge, and correct AI-mediated decisions that affect their
access to public or community services?

## Why It Matters

An explanation is not meaningful recourse if no one can correct the decision or accept
responsibility. Research should examine whether affected people can exercise agency,
including those with limited time, language access, or technical familiarity.

## Hypothesis

Evidence-linked explanations paired with a named human review route will improve
successful error correction over explanation-only interfaces. They may fail when
reviewers lack authority or access barriers remain.

## Proposed Method

- Create synthetic eligibility, referral, and resource-allocation scenarios with
  documented rules and deliberately seeded errors; use no real benefit decisions.
- Compare explanation-only, appeal-form, and accountable human-review workflows with
  consenting adult participants and service practitioners.
- Measure recognition of errors, ability to contest, correction accuracy, time and
  effort, accessibility barriers, and unresolved cases. Separate user comprehension from
  actual institutional correction.

## Needed Controls

- Obtain appropriate ethics review and consent. Avoid recruiting people in ways that
  imply participation affects services.
- Include affected-community input and language/accessibility needs. Disclose contested
  policy assumptions instead of presenting administrative rules as inherently
  legitimate.
- Ensure comparison reviewers have specified authority and matched evidence. Report
  burdens and failures, not only average completion rates.

## Relationship to Existing Ideas

Extends FID-038's concerns about consequential profiling and FID-064's collective
judgment questions to practical contestability and institutional correction.

## Outputs

- A contestability evaluation protocol and synthetic service scenarios.
- A design-and-governance report connecting explanations to enforceable correction
  routes.

## Open Questions

- Who bears responsibility when a model, vendor, and institution share a workflow?
- When should a service decline automation even if its explanations are understandable?

## Ways to Help

- Public-service and nonprofit practitioners to model realistic review routes.
- Affected-community representatives and accessibility researchers to define meaningful
  recourse.

## Public Claim Boundary

This is a seed proposal, not an evaluation of a named public institution or evidence
that automated eligibility decisions are appropriate.
