# FID-084: Learning, Agency, and Relationships in AI-Assisted Education

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `human-agency-formation`

Additional areas: `high-trust-domains`

Application domains: `education-and-youth`, `families-and-households`

Primary need: education research, developmental expertise, human factors, ethics review

## Question

Which forms of AI assistance improve immediate task performance while preserving
independent judgment, durable learning, and appropriate human relationships?

## Why It Matters

Helpful answers and positive user ratings do not establish educational benefit.
Assistance may support or substitute for the effort and relationships through which
people learn to judge, seek help, and act independently.

## Hypothesis

Scaffolded assistance that asks learners to reason and consult appropriate people will
improve unaided transfer compared with answer-first assistance. It may provide no
benefit or disadvantage learners with different access needs.

## Proposed Method

- Begin with adult learners and synthetic family-advice scenarios; compare no-AI,
  answer-first, and scaffolded assistance with matched learning objectives.
- Measure immediate performance, delayed unaided retention, transfer to unfamiliar
  tasks, error correction, appropriate help-seeking, and reliance calibration.
- Co-design a separate youth protocol with educators, developmental specialists, and
  family representatives before considering research with minors.

## Needed Controls

- Pre-register outcomes and distinguish satisfaction, time saved, learning, and
  well-being. Account for prior knowledge and accessibility needs.
- Require ethics review and informed consent; no covert monitoring of family
  relationships. Any youth study needs appropriate guardian consent and participant
  assent.
- Disclose assumptions about autonomy, beneficial learning, and family authority; do not
  treat one household structure or educational tradition as universal.

## Relationship to Existing Ideas

Broadens the learning and agency questions while preserving FID-016 and FID-050 as
distinct faith-context calls.

## Outputs

- A longitudinal learning-and-agency protocol and safe task materials.
- An analysis of performance versus durable-learning tradeoffs, including heterogeneous
  effects.

## Open Questions

- How long must follow-up last to detect meaningful dependence or skill change?
- Which relational outcomes can be responsibly measured without intrusive observation?

## Ways to Help

- Educators, developmental researchers, and family representatives to co-design
  outcomes.
- Learning scientists and accessibility specialists to review the protocol.

## Public Claim Boundary

A proposed study, not evidence of educational benefit or harm. Adult or simulated
results do not establish effects on children or family relationships.
