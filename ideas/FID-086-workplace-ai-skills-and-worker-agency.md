# FID-086: Workplace AI, Durable Skills, and Worker Agency

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `work-common-good`

Additional areas: `human-agency-formation`, `governance-readiness`

Application domains: `workplace-and-enterprise`, `families-and-households`

Primary need: labor research, organizational studies, worker participation, longitudinal evaluation

## Question

How does AI-mediated work change workers' skills, decision authority, and ability to adapt, beyond short-term productivity gains?

## Why It Matters

Task throughput can rise while useful judgment, bargaining power, or opportunities to learn decline. A trustworthy-AI research agenda should distinguish benefits to an organization from benefits and burdens experienced by workers and their households.

## Hypothesis

Worker-controlled assistance with protected learning opportunities will better preserve independent performance than automation optimized only for throughput. Effects may depend on role, prior skill, and how gains are distributed.

## Proposed Method

- Start with consenting adult participants in simulated work tasks; compare unaided, automation-led, and worker-controlled assistance with matched objectives.
- Measure output quality and time alongside delayed unaided performance, ability to detect errors, decision discretion, learning opportunities, and perceived pressure.
- Scope a separate longitudinal organizational study with worker participation to examine role transitions and distribution of benefits; avoid inferring livelihood effects from task experiments alone.

## Needed Controls

- No covert employee surveillance or individual performance reporting to employers. Establish consent, confidentiality, and withdrawal protections.
- Account for prior expertise, job security, accessibility, and selection effects. Pre-register subgroup analyses and follow-up periods.
- Disclose the definition of useful work and whose interests inform success measures. Separate perceived autonomy from actual decision rights.

## Relationship to Existing Ideas

Preserves FID-046 through FID-050 as faith-context work and extends the broader questions of useful contribution, durable skills, and worker agency.

## Outputs

- A worker-agency and durable-skills assessment protocol.
- A research plan for longitudinal effects and a report of simulation limits.

## Open Questions

- Which changes in skill and discretion persist after assistance is removed?
- How can workers help govern the study and benefit from its outputs?

## Ways to Help

- Workers, labor researchers, and organizational scholars to co-design outcomes.
- Employers and public-interest partners willing to support independent, consent-based research.

## Public Claim Boundary

A seed proposal, not a forecast of job displacement or proof of AI's effects on livelihoods. Simulated productivity results cannot establish household or economy-wide outcomes.
